Smart financing

Build more, spend less — fund it the right way.

How you pay for a project can change what you can afford. Here's the honest landscape — then we design your project to fit the path you choose.

Please note: RHDP17 is a drafting & design firm — not a lender or financial advisor. This is general education only. Always confirm current rates, terms, and what fits your situation with your bank or a licensed professional.
Path A

Construction loan

  • Funds a build in stages ("draws"), often converts to a mortgage
  • Lender inspections at each draw
  • Tends to carry higher rates, more fees, more paperwork
  • Interest-only during the build is common
Best for: ground-up or large new builds.
Path B

Renovation / equity loan

  • Uses your existing home equity — HELOC, cash-out refinance, or renovation loans (FHA 203k, Fannie HomeStyle)
  • Often simpler and lower-cost than a construction loan
  • Fewer draw/inspection hoops
Best for: remodels, additions, and ADUs.

Why it often saves money

For many remodels, additions, and ADUs, a renovation or equity-based loan can avoid the higher cost and complexity of a full construction loan. Whether it's right for you depends on your equity, credit, and project size — your bank is the one to confirm the numbers.
