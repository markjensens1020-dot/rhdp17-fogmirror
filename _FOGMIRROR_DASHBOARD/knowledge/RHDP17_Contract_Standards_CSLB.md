## DEPOSIT / DOWN PAYMENT — BPC 7159.5(a)(3)  *(hard limit)*
- Contracts **$10,000 or more** → max deposit **$1,000**
- Contracts **under $10,000** → max deposit **10%** of the total
- This applies to **any** upfront ask before work begins — down payment, retainer, **or an initial invoice**.
- Revised deposit figures go to **Richard and Irma** for final review before anything is sent to a client.

## EVERY DOCUMENT
- The **Contractor's License # (704179)** must appear on **every** Proposal/Estimate, Contract, and Invoice/Change Order.

## CONTRACT REQUIREMENTS — BPC 7159
- **Written agreement**, signed by contractor and homeowner **before any work begins** (incl. all change/extra-work orders).
- **Basic disclosures:** contractor name, business address, license #, date signed.
- **Schedule of progress payments:** payments must match work actually performed / materials delivered — no collecting ahead of the work (except the allowed down payment).
- **Notice of Cancellation** section near the signature lines (homeowner's right to cancel).
- **Format:** text ≥ 10-point; major headings 10-point boldface. State the **total cost** in dollars and cents (excluding separate finance charges).

## DESIGN RETAINER — legal basis (researched July 6 2026; not legal advice)
- Clarifies the "retainer" note above: the §7159.5 cap applies to **home-improvement / construction** contracts. A **design agreement is a SEPARATE professional-services contract** and is **NOT a home-improvement contract under §7159 — so it is NOT subject to the $1,000 / 10% cap.** A designer may charge a legitimate **design retainer** (schematic design, construction documents, permit prep) under its own written agreement.
- So RHDP17's **25% retainer, first and separate, is legal** under a standalone **Design Services Agreement** — no bond required — as long as it stays design-only and is NOT folded into a construction deposit.
- Keep-it-clean conditions (RHDP17 already follows): design/drafting only; **structural via the licensed engineer of record**; the **designer signs the plans**; the firm **never holds out as "architect"** (Architects Practice Act).
- The cap **still applies to any separate CONSTRUCTION contract.** A CSLB-approved performance/payment bond exempts a contractor from the cap on construction contracts.
- Deliverable: `_FORMS/Contracts/RHDP17_Design_Services_Agreement.docx` (black-and-white). Have design-only treatment confirmed by the legal service (Rocket Lawyer attorney review).
- Sources: BPC §7159 / §7159.5 / §7151 (Justia/FindLaw); CSLB Progress-Payment Bulletin (2022).

## ADDITIONS / BIG JOBS / NEW BUILDS — deposit rules (researched July 6 2026; not legal advice)
- **An ADDITION (adding square footage to an existing home) = "home improvement"** — §7151 literally includes "adding to" residential property. So it IS subject to the **$1,000 / 10% (whichever less) down-payment cap.** A high contract price ($800k–$1.5M) does NOT exempt it for a homeowner client.
- **New single-family home construction (original) = §7164** — carries the SAME "$1,000 or 10%, whichever less" down-payment statement for a homeowner.
- **CRITICAL nuance:** the cap is ONLY on the **up-front down payment**, NOT the total and NOT progress payments. On big jobs you still collect the whole contract via a **schedule of progress payments in dollars-and-cents tied to work performed** (foundation, framing, etc.) — you just can't collect ahead of the work beyond the deposit.
- **Three legal ways to take a large payment up front on residential:**
  1. **Post a CSLB-approved bond** (blanket performance/payment bond, or lien+completion bond) → **exempt from the cap** → can take a real mobilization deposit. (Talk to a surety/bond agent.)
  2. **Commercial / developer / investor client (not the owner-occupant of a residence)** → **NOT a home-improvement contract → NO deposit cap.** Negotiate freely.
  3. **Design portion** → separate **Design Services Agreement** (uncapped 25%+ retainer) — already built.
- San José / Santa Clara County: no extra local deposit rule — this is statewide CSLB law.
- Confirm the bond route with a surety agent + Rocket Lawyer attorney review before relying on it.
- Sources: BPC §7164, §7159.5, §7151; CSLB Progress-Payment Restrictions Bulletin (2022).
